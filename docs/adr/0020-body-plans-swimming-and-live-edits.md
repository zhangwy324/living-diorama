# ADR 0020: Body plans, swimming and live world edits

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-28
- **Context:** Roadmap §3.2 step 7 pairs drawing release 2 (a body plan for drawn creatures, swimming, an animation per way of moving) with live world edits, because both meet in capabilities, water regions and connections. The step is split in two milestones. **M16** builds body plans, swimming, hopping and a simple animation per locomotion mode. **M17** builds live world edits. This ADR records the decisions for both, agreed with the owner on 2026-10-28: M16's to build now, M17's contracts fixed now and built later. M16 lands as a series of PRs: **0** this ADR; **1** the schema: the body plan (template, land gait, anchors) on the species, library entries version 3, the capability's animation profile and submersion (§1, §2, §9–§11, §13); **2** the simulation: `core:walk` renamed `core:land`, `core:swim` added, movement in water, the derived way into water, speed per medium, the animation profile and submersion in the snapshot, the invariants, the relaxed startup warnings, protocol 11 and a smooth turn when setting off (§2–§5, §7–§9); **3** content: a water spawn point in Woodland Hollow, and any shore exits it still wants (§4, §6); **4** the renderer: whole-body animation per profile, anchors as pivots and the submersion offset (§8–§10); **5** the Studio's confirm panel (§12); **6** app wiring: the body plan replaces the drawn species' fixed capabilities (§14); **7** headless swim and hop scenarios, soak, the manual checklist and `docs/development.md`. PR 1 comes first; PRs 2, 3 and 4 can then run in parallel; PRs 5, 6 and 7 follow in order. Each PR adds its own paragraph under "As built" below where it departs from this ADR. All M16 PRs have landed (#123, #124, #129, #130, #134, #135 and #137), so the M16 decisions below, as amended, describe the code. The owner's hand check (the "Swimming, hopping and animation" checklist in [development](../development.md) and the Studio's confirm-step lines) passed on 2026-10-29. It used the Studio with the Body section (walker, hopper, swimmer), which a follow-up removes so that the ways of moving alone decide. One new finding is accepted as a follow-up: a very long creature that turns across the river is longer than the river is wide, so its head or tail clips into a bank (#142). The known issues #132, #138, #140 and #141 are accepted too.

This ADR amends ADR 0004 §1 and ADR 0005 §3 (`core:walk` becomes `core:land`; hopping is a gait, not a capability), ADR 0005 §4 and §5 (walkability and startup warnings per capability), ADR 0006 §9 and §12 (heading when setting off, the water invariants), ADR 0014 §11 (drawn species defaults) and ADR 0015 §8 (`check_limits` asks with each species' own capabilities, not one fixed set), each once the PR named there lands. It resolves five places where the spec and the ADRs disagree (see "Contradictions resolved").

## Decisions

### Locomotion (M16)

1. **Capabilities say where a creature can be; a style says how it moves there.** There are two locomotion capabilities: land movement and swimming. Swimming is a new capability, `core:swim`, not a mode that makes every swimmer amphibious. A species has land movement, swimming or both, and at least one.

   How a creature moves within a medium is a separate choice, made per medium in the body plan (§11). On land it is the **gait**: walk or hop, never both, used only when the species has land movement. A gait is a style: it picks the animation profile on land (§8), and may later tune speed or rhythm. It is not a capability, so connections never require a gait, navigation never sees one, and a walker and a hopper of the same size share a navigation profile.

   **Water styles later.** M16 has a single way of swimming: the creature moves on the water's surface plane, lowered by its submersion (§9). There is no water depth and no vertical movement. Water creatures will later get styles of their own, parallel to the land gait: swimming submerged like a fish, floating at the surface, or both. That is the extension path: a water style chosen per medium in the body plan beside the land gait, mapped to a profile and a submersion, which the snapshot already carries. No field for it is added until it is built.

   Water-only creatures have no food or shelter yet. That is accepted for M16: water food and shelter come later. Until then a water-only creature's hunger rises and its hungry cue shows, it sleeps where it is (ADR 0018 §13's fallback), and nothing dies (sanctuary mode).

2. **The land capability becomes `core:land`; `core:swim` is a registry entry, not engine code** (ADR 0005 §3).
   - `core:walk` is renamed `core:land` in PR 2, so the capability isn't confused with the walk gait. Its locomotion rule is unchanged (`land`, `cave`, 35°). Every reference moves with it (the mossling, connections, drawn species, tests and docs). No backward compatibility is required (AGENTS.md), so there is no alias.
   - `core:swim`: `regionKinds: [water]`, no slope limit (the water surface is flat).
   - There is no `core:hop`: hopping is a gait (§1).
   - A capability gains an optional animation profile (§8). `core:swim` declares `swim`. `core:land` declares that the species' land gait chooses its profile, so the engine resolves it to `walk` or `hop` from the species' data without naming either capability.

   A hop is a way of drawing the creature, not a way of moving it: the simulation moves a hopper along its path as smoothly as a walker, and the renderer draws the hops (§8). Speed per medium needs nothing new: ADR 0006 §9 already moves a creature at `baseSpeed` times the largest factor among the capabilities whose rule covers the current region's kind, so an amphibious creature moves at its `core:land` factor on land and its `core:swim` factor in water.

3. **Walkability in water follows the rules that already exist** (ADR 0005 §4), applied per profile. A water region's cells are walkable for a profile whose locomotion covers `water`, on the region's flat surface, subject to clearance like any region. A cell of a water region counts only where its surface lies above the ground, the mirror of ADR 0005 §4's _submerged_ rule, so a water footprint drawn over the bank doesn't make dry ground swimmable. Land under water stays submerged and blocked for land regions, for swimmers too. PR 2 records any detail it needs at the bank.

4. **In and out of the water across any bank low and near enough; no connection needed.** _Revised by the owner on 2026-10-28, after PR 2 first built a one-way entry with authored exits only (see "As built", PR 2)._
   - **Both ways, derived.** A creature that can both swim and move on land may jump between land and water anywhere along a bank, into the water and back out, where the jump is small enough. Banks are derived from the terrain when navigation is built and need no connection in the world content. A bank joins the region-level search (ADR 0006 §6) like a crossing, with the in-region costs worked out the same way; it has no `structureId`, so it never fires `first_use_of_structure`.
   - **Small enough** is one fixed limit for every creature: the land and water surfaces are at most about 0.75 m apart vertically, the same limit for climbing out as for jumping in, and the horizontal gap between them is small. PR 2 sets the gap and records it.
   - **Authored shore and beach exits still work** and remain allowed (connections requiring `core:swim`, one-way or two-way), but they are optional: a world needs one only where no bank is low and near enough. A water-only species can't use an exit, because the land side is in a region kind it can't move in, so no special rule keeps it in the water; its checks skip the exit (§5).
   - For a profile that can swim and move on land, the region graph is two-way at banks; it stays directed where a connection is one-way. For the seam check (`region_seam`, ADR 0005 §5), walkable land and water cells that touch within a bank's limits count as joined; otherwise every bank would warn of an invisible wall.
   - A world whose water has every bank too high or too far, and no exit, would keep an amphibious creature that got in there (by a one-way connection, or by appearing at a spawn point in the water) for good. PR 2 adds a per-profile check for a water region the profile can get into but not leave, `no_water_exit`. It runs wherever a profile is checked (`checkNavigation`, at registration and for `check_limits`). It is a limit, a warning the Studio shows as a hint (ADR 0013 §4, ADR 0015 §8), not an error: the species can still be released.
   - _Superseded:_ the first version of this decision had a one-way derived entry into the water and authored exits as the only way out.

5. **The startup warnings no longer assume today's species.** With swimmers drawn in the Studio, the species that use an exit or a water spawn point are registered from the library after startup, if at all, so warnings keyed on the species registered at startup would fire on every launch of a correct world.
   - **`unsatisfiable_connection` is removed** (ADR 0005 §5). Reference resolution already rejects a connection whose `requiredCapabilities` name an unregistered capability before `checkWorld` runs (ADR 0005 §2), so the only case left is a registered capability no species has yet, which is exactly the swimmer case.
   - **`spawn_unusable` is relaxed:** it fires only for a spawn point in a region whose kind no registered capability's locomotion rule covers, whatever species are registered.

   **Per-profile checks treat a medium the profile can't move in as not applicable.** When a profile is checked (`checkNavigation`, at registration and for `check_limits`), a connection one of whose sides is in a region kind the profile has no locomotion for is skipped, as a connection needing a capability the profile lacks is (`missing_capability`). It is not reported as `no_walkable_side` and gives no `portal_unusable` limit. So a water-only swimmer gets no hint about shore exits, and a walker none about them either.

6. **A water-only species needs somewhere to appear.** A profile that fits no spawn point can't be registered (ADR 0012 §4, `no_spawn_point`), and Woodland Hollow's spawn points are all on land. PR 3 adds at least one spawn point in the river, and spawning a creature picks only spawn points its profile can stand on, as it does now.

### Heading (M16)

7. **Setting off turns smoothly** (issue #121, folded into PR 2 because it touches the same movement and heading code). Walking no longer snaps the heading to the direction of travel the moment a creature sets off; it turns with ADR 0018's turn rate, as eating and sleeping already do. Whether the creature turns before moving or while moving, and how sharp corners are eased, is PR 2's choice. Runs with the same seed stay identical, and a save taken mid-turn restores the same turn.

### Animation and drawing (M16)

8. **Whole-body animation only, kept easy to replace.** Procedural animation and per-part skeletons come later and will replace what M16 builds, so M16 keeps it small:
   - a profile moves the whole body: squash and stretch, tilt, bob, and a sway or undulation made from these. No skinned meshes, no bones, no vertex-shader bending;
   - three profiles: `walk` (a gait: a small bob and tilt in step with speed), `hop` (squash before take-off, an arc, squash on landing) and `swim` (a side-to-side sway or undulation plus a bob);
   - each profile is a small function from its inputs (the profile, the creature's speed, its own phase, the elapsed time) to a body transform. The renderer calls all of them through one interface, so swapping the whole approach later touches one module;
   - profiles combine with ADR 0018's cues (the eating bob, the sleeping squash) rather than replace them; PR 4 decides how they add up;
   - a profile names an entry in the renderer's profile table; an unknown or missing profile draws no motion, as an asset the renderer can't load draws a neutral fallback (ADR 0007 §1).

   The snapshot carries each creature's animation profile, taken from the capability in use (the one ADR 0006 §9 picks for the current region): `swim` in water, and on land the profile of the species' gait. The renderer reads profile names and never capability IDs, region kinds or action IDs.

9. **Swimmers sit lower in water.** The snapshot carries a submersion value per creature, a share of the body's height to lower it by, which the renderer applies as an offset. It comes from the capability in use: a capability may declare `submersion` (0 to 1, default 0), and `core:swim` declares one. The renderer never interprets region kinds; it just lowers the body.

10. **Anchors are optional, few, and in drawing pixels.** _Amended by the owner on 2026-10-28, after PRs 5 and 6: the walker's `feet` became the center of gravity, and the hopper has one too (see "As built", Follow-up: center of gravity). Amended again by the owner on 2026-10-29: there are no templates; every species may have both anchors, each optional, and the Studio shows the center of gravity for land movement and the tail for swimming (see "As built", Follow-up: no body templates)._ A template has a few named anchors: a walker and a hopper have a `centerOfGravity`, a swimmer a `tail`. Each defaults to a point taken from the drawing's bounds (the center of gravity at the middle of the drawn shape's bounds; the tail at the middle of the bounds' left edge, the rear, since drawings face right, ADR 0011 §7), and the player may move it in the confirm panel (§12). The center of gravity is where the body balances: the player may put it anywhere on the body, a point in the middle of the torso included, and only its left–right position is used, since the body rocks and squashes about the point on the ground below it (where the body meets the ground, ADR 0011 §3). It is not found from where the legs touch the ground, since a creature's legs needn't end at one height. Anchors are stored in the drawing's pixels in `species.body`, on the image, which is the drawing (ADR 0017 §8). M16's profiles may use them as pivots (tilting and squashing about the ground below the center of gravity, swaying about the tail); otherwise they are kept for the per-part animation that replaces M16's.

### The body plan (M16)

11. **The species carries its body plan.** _Amended by the owner on 2026-10-29: `template` is removed. The ways of moving alone decide behaviour, and the confirm panel starts from land movement with the walk gait (see "As built", Follow-up: no body templates)._ `species.body` (ADR 0017 §11, today only `thickness`) gains:
    - `template`: `walker`, `hopper` or `swimmer` for now, with more later. The template chooses which anchors apply and what the confirm panel starts from (walker: land movement with the walk gait; hopper: land movement with the hop gait; swimmer: swimming only). The player can then add or remove swimming or land movement, and switch the gait, within §1's rules;
    - the style per medium: for now only the land gait, `walk` or `hop`, used when the species has land movement and walk by default. A water style joins it later (§1);
    - `anchors`: the template's anchors that the player placed or accepted, by name, as `{ x, y }` in drawing pixels.

    Where a species can move stays where it is, in the species' `capabilities` map (ADR 0004 §2): land movement and swimming are `core:land` and `core:swim` entries with their speed factors. The gait is not in that map, and nothing is stored twice. PR 1 chooses the field names, but the style should be shaped per medium, for example `locomotion: { land: { gait } }`, so a water style can later sit beside it (`locomotion.water.style`) without renaming anything.

12. **A confirm step in the Studio.** _Amended by the owner on 2026-10-29: the panel has no template; the points shown follow the ways of moving (see "As built", Follow-up: no body templates)._ Between drawing and Release, the Studio shows a panel: the template, land movement and swimming, the land gait, clicks to place the anchors, and an animated preview of the body in each way it moves. The size limits (ADR 0015 §8) are asked for the chosen capabilities, since a navigation profile is the size class plus the capabilities (ADR 0012); the gait doesn't change them. The release moment (#51) and the size suggestion (#49) stay separate issues.

13. **Library entries go to version 3.** _Amended on 2026-10-29: version 4 drops the template, and a species without a body plan has the walk gait and default anchors (see "As built", Follow-up: no body templates)._ The body plan is stored with every species the library saves. An entry or a species without a body plan is read as a walker with default anchors. That is a cheap default for content with no plan, not a migration path: no backward compatibility is required (AGENTS.md), and entries of version 2 may simply fail to load.

14. **The body plan replaces the drawn species' fixed capabilities.** ADR 0014 §11's `DRAWN_SPECIES_DEFAULTS` gave every drawn species `core:walk` 1. From PR 6 a drawn species takes its capabilities (`core:land`, `core:swim` or both) and its gait from its body plan, with a speed factor of 1 for each capability unless PR 1 or PR 6 tunes it. The other defaults (base speed, behaviors, senses, traits) stay app configuration until drawing release 3 reads them from the drawing (roadmap §3.2 step 8).

15. **Out of scope:** changing the body plan of a species already released (ADR 0015: a redraw is a new species; editing comes later).

### Live world edits (M17; fixed now, built later)

16. **What an edit can do.** M17's edits add, remove and move prop and structure placements. No terrain edits yet. They are made through a development hook in the app and through `pnpm sim`; a player-facing tool comes with structure drawing.

17. **One edit path, in the simulation.** Every world edit goes through a single validated edit command in the worker, applied between ticks like every other command (ADR 0006 §11). Nothing else changes world content at run time. The Studio, the dev hook, the headless runner and later structure drawing and the agent bridge all reach the world through it.

18. **A save stores edits, not a new world.** A save keeps an ordered list of the edits applied since the base world, and a restore replays them onto the base world before restoring the creatures. Edits never bump the world's version (ADR 0003); the base world's ID and version still decide whether a save matches (ADR 0019 §7). The save schema's version goes up when the list is added.
    - **A replayed edit that fails is skipped.** If an edit no longer applies on restore (its placement or type is gone, or it fails validation), it is skipped with a warning event and in the restore report, and the edits after it still apply. Edits after it that refer to a placement it would have added fail and are skipped the same way.
    - **Placements keep their IDs.** An add-placement edit carries the new placement's ID, chosen when the edit is made, so later edits, inverse edits and replays all refer to the same placement.
    - **Storage stays JSON.** The edit list lives in `world.json` with the rest of the save, which closes the question ADR 0019 §1 left open for this step: no SQLite and no `persistence` package. SQLite can be revisited if edit lists grow large enough that writing the save whole costs too much.

19. **A trapped creature is moved, never deleted.** When an edit leaves a creature off any walkable cell of its profile (a bridge removed under it, a prop placed on it), the engine moves it to the nearest valid cell in a region's main walkable area (the region's largest connected piece, so it isn't put on an island it can't leave): its own region's if that region still exists, else the nearest region its profile can move in. It emits an event saying so. The edit is rejected only when no such cell exists. A creature is never deleted. Routes that cross the edited area are replanned. Home and knowledge entries naming a removed placement are pruned as a restore prunes them (ADR 0019 §7).

    **Blocking an exit out of water is allowed, with a warning.** An edit that blocks one or more water→land exits, even the last one out of a water region, still applies; the result warns and names each blocked exit. Exits here are the derived banks (§4) as well as authored connections. Amphibious creatures in that water stay there until an exit opens again. Rejection is kept only for a creature trapped inside new solid geometry with no valid cell to move to (above).

20. **Navigation rebuilds whole regions first.** An edit rebuilds the walkability masks, crossings and distance fields of every region it touches, for every profile, extending ADR 0006 §6's `invalidateRegions`. Finer, dirty-rectangle rebuilds wait until measurement shows whole regions are too slow.

21. **Validate first; return the inverse.** The edit command has a dry-run `validate` that reports, without changing anything:
    - whether the edit would be accepted;
    - which creatures it would move;
    - preview data: the cells the edited placement would occupy (its footprint) and the water→land exits it would block.

    The preview data is a contract for a later edit UI, which can show how much room a prop takes and highlight a blocked exit while the player places it. That preview UI is built with the player edit UI (structure drawing); M17 provides only the data in the `validate` result, with no dev overlay. An applied edit returns its inverse edit, so an undo is one more edit. A player-facing undo comes later.

## Contradictions resolved

The spec predates several ADRs. Where they disagree, this ADR settles it; the private design docs are updated to match.

- **Strokes or image as the source of truth.** The spec's creature flow and asset list keep vector strokes as the editable original. Since ADR 0017 §8 the 256 px image is the drawing and the strokes are a record that is never replayed. That stands, and anchors (§10) are stored in the image's pixels.
- **The template list.** _Amended by the owner on 2026-10-29: there are no templates; a creature's ways of moving and land gait say what it is (see "As built", Follow-up: no body templates)._ The spec lists eight creature body plans and names a different first set in its open questions. The templates are walker, hopper and swimmer for now (§11); others (flyer, crawler and so on) come later. A walker and a hopper differ in gait, not in capability.
- **What animation deforms.** The spec's baseline is a 2D billboard animated with squash, sway, bob and limb anchors. Since ADR 0011 the body is an extruded mesh, and M16 animates that mesh as a whole (§8). The billboard is gone.
- **The water invariants.** ADR 0006 §12 checks that every creature stands on a non-water region and never under a water surface. That was true while only walkers existed. The invariants now hold per capability: each creature is on a walkable cell of a region its capabilities' locomotion covers, on that region's surface, and changes region only through a connection joining the two or by a jump across a bank, either way between land and water (§4). A walker still never enters water; a swimmer is in water only on a water region's surface.
- **How the Studio commits to the world.** The spec has the Studio commit through a world-edit interface, while the app has the Studio ask the strip, which sends worker commands (ADR 0015). For creatures nothing changes: a release registers a species and spawns creatures, which are not world edits. For world edits M17 settles it: the one edit path is the worker's edit command (§17), and the Studio reaches it through the strip like any other command.

## Exit

M16 is done when:

- a drawn species can be a walker, hopper or swimmer, with swimming on or off for walkers and hoppers and land movement (walking or hopping) on or off for swimmers; _amended on 2026-10-29: it moves on land (walking or hopping), swims, or both, with no template;_
- `core:walk` is renamed `core:land` everywhere, and there is no hop capability;
- an amphibious creature jumps into the river from the bank and climbs out on a bank low enough, or by a shore exit; a water-only creature stays in the water; a walker never enters it;
- walking, hopping and swimming each show their own animation, and a swimmer sits lower in the water;
- creatures turn smoothly when they set off (#121);
- the Studio's confirm panel sets the template, locomotion and anchors and previews the animation; _amended on 2026-10-29: locomotion and the anchors it uses, with no template;_
- `pnpm check` and `pnpm soak` pass with swim and hop scenarios and the per-capability invariants;
- a manual checklist entry is added and run by the owner;
- this ADR records the decisions and every departure from them.

M17's exit is set when it starts.

## As built

Each M16 PR adds its own paragraph here, in its own slot, so PRs landing in parallel don't conflict.

### PR 1: schema

- **The body plan is `species.body`** (`BodyShapeSchema` in `content-schema`; the name stays `BodyShape`, so the protocol and renderer types carry the plan unchanged). Every field has a default, and `defaultBody()` returns a walker:
  - `template`: `"walker" | "hopper" | "swimmer"`, default `"walker"` (`BODY_TEMPLATES`). It is kept separate from locomotion because it chooses the anchors and what the confirm panel starts from, which the capabilities and gait don't say (a swimmer that can also move on land still has a tail).
  - `locomotion: { land: { gait: "walk" | "hop" } }`, always present after parsing, gait default `"walk"` (`LAND_GAITS`). There is no `water` key: §1 adds none until a water style is built, and it will sit beside `land` as `locomotion.water`. `LocomotionMedium` (now `"land"`) and `LOCOMOTION_MEDIA` are derived from these keys. A swimmer-only species still carries a land gait, unused, so the confirm panel keeps the choice if land movement is switched back on.
  - `anchors: { feet?: { x, y }, tail?: { x, y } }`, default `{}`, in drawing pixels from the top left, each coordinate from 0 to `DRAWING_SIZE` (256, now the one constant behind the Studio's `CANVAS_SIZE` and the renderer's `BODY_IMAGE_SIZE`). Only the template's anchors are allowed (`BODY_TEMPLATE_ANCHORS`: walker `feet`, swimmer `tail`, hopper none). An anchor left out is placed by `defaultAnchor(name, bounds)` from the drawn shape's bounds (left and top inclusive, right and bottom exclusive), which PR 4 and PR 5 share.
- **"At least one medium" is checked on the capabilities**, not in the body plan, since where a species can move lives only there (§11): a species whose capabilities include no locomotion rule fails reference resolution with `no_locomotion`. It runs in `resolveReferences` and `addEntries` through a new entry-wide check (`RegistryOptions.check`), because no single reference can make it.
- **A capability's animation and submersion sit on its locomotion rule** (`LocomotionRule` in `extension-api`), since both only mean something for a way of moving: `animation?: string | { fromStyle: LocomotionMedium }` and `submersion?: number` (0 to 1, a share of the body's height, 0 if omitted). PR 2 gives `core:land` `animation: { fromStyle: "land" }` and `core:swim` `animation: "swim"` with a submersion. `animationProfile(rule, body)` resolves the profile name: the string, the species' gait for `{ fromStyle: "land" }` (each gait is a profile of the same name), or undefined for none. A water style later only adds a case there; the switch is exhaustive, so a new medium doesn't compile until it does. Submersion may likewise widen to `number | { fromStyle }` when water styles arrive (a fish deeper than a floater), so PR 2 should read it through one helper rather than treat it as a plain number in several places.
- **No capability-level speed factor.** The species' `capabilities` map already gives each capability its speed factor, and ADR 0006 §9 picks the factor per region (§2), so a drawn species' factor of 1 per capability is PR 6's to set there.
- **Library entries are version 3** and are not migrated: entries of versions 1 and 2 fail to load with `missing_migration`. The version 1 migration (ADR 0018's feeding, resting and vision) is removed with its tests, as it could no longer complete. The species definition stays at schema version 1, since its new fields all have defaults.

### PR 2: simulation

- **Capabilities.** `core:walk` is now `core:land` everywhere, with no alias; its rule gains `animation: { fromStyle: "land" }`. `core:swim` covers `water` with no slope limit, `animation: "swim"` and `submersion` 0.5 (`SWIM_SUBMERSION`), so a swimmer sits half its height low. Submersion is read only through `locomotionSubmersion(rule)` in `extension-api`, beside `animationProfile`; it takes the species' body when it widens to `{ fromStyle }`.
- **Water walkability (§3).** A water region's cell is walkable for a swimmer only where its surface lies above the terrain all over the cell (its centre and four points just inside its corners), since a creature may stand anywhere in a walkable cell; elsewhere it is a new state, `dry`, which keeps a swimmer's radius away, so its body stays out of the bank. Land is `submerged` likewise if water lies above it anywhere in the cell, not only at its centre, so no creature wades with its centre under the surface where the waterline crosses a cell. Submerged land stays unwalkable for every profile, but for a profile that can swim it is no obstacle for clearance: water is somewhere it can go, so its walkable land reaches the waterline. The engine now checks the `water` region kind for these rules (ADR 0005 §3 is amended to say so).
- **Banks (§4, as revised).** `Navigation.banks` lists, per land and water region pair, for each walkable cell of a region the profile moves in (any kind but `water`) the nearest walkable cell of the water region (the first in cell order on a tie) that it can jump to: their surfaces at most `maxBankJumpHeight` (0.75 m) apart, both ways alike, and their centres at most `bankReach` apart horizontally, which is twice the creature's radius plus `maxBankJumpGap` (1.75 m) between its body's edges on either side. Both limits are in `WORLD_TOLERANCES` and `docs/world-coordinates.md`. Banks are links of the region graph both ways; the graph machinery stays directed for one-way connections. The pathfinder turns each bank into crossings like a portal's, both ways: per bank and pair of walkable areas it joins, cell pairs whose land cells are at least 2 m apart, in cell order, so every area that reaches the water has a way in and out and the door count stays bounded.
- **A jump needs a clear line, and is made whole in one tick.** A pair counts only if the straight line between the two cell centres is clear, checked every 0.1 m: no prop's block radius comes within the creature's radius of it, the ground along it rises no more than `maxStepHeight` above the higher end, and no other surface (a bridge deck, say) lies across it between the lower end and the creature's height above the higher one. Water surfaces don't count, nor do regions whose surface is the ground there. Where the nearest water cell's line is blocked, the nearest one with a clear line is taken. A creature small enough passes under a deck with room for its height, as walkability lets it stand under one. The cells along the line can be ones no creature may stand on (the steep bank face), so the walk moves the creature from one end to the other in a tick of its own: the jump starts the tick, from where the walk stopped on the last one, and ends it. The creature is always on a walkable cell, and a route planned after an interruption always starts from one. The renderer's interpolation shows it as a quick glide over 0.1 s; a hop arc is the renderer's (#132). A jump's crossing, path waypoint and `entered_region` event have no `connectionId`, and it never fires `first_use_of_structure`.
- **The gap, sized on Woodland Hollow.** Its banks slope at 45–55°, and the waterline falls on that steep face, 0.65–0.75 m below the walkable bank top. The walkable land stops short of the water by the bank face (about 1 m) plus the creature's clearance from it, and walkable water likewise keeps its radius from the bank, so the centres are 1–1.5 m apart for tiny and small creatures, a median of 2.5 m for medium and 3.5 m for large. With a gap of 1.75 m, every size can get into and out of the river along both banks without reshaping it: tiny and small at 28 of 33 one-metre rows of the west bank and all 34 of the east, medium at 22 of 31 and 25 of 33, large at 4 of 27 and 6 of 31 (the rest are higher than 0.75 m or have no room). A gap of 1.5 m would leave medium creatures, the drawn default, only 5 and 6 rows; 2 m would give medium 26 and 29, large 13 and 18. 1.75 m is the smallest that serves the default size well.
- **The first version of this PR** built a one-way derived entry into the water, edge to edge within a step, and authored exits as the only way out. On the Hollow it found no entries at all, which led to the owner's revision of §4.
- **The capability in use** in a region kind is the one with the largest speed factor among those covering it (ADR 0006 §9), the first by ID on a tie, whatever order the species lists them in. It gives the speed, and the snapshot's `animation` (`animationProfile`) and `submersion` (0 where it declares none), cached per species and kind. Each creature in the snapshot has both fields, as PR 4 expects; the protocol is version 11, which also makes `entered_region.connectionId` optional.
- **Startup and per-profile checks (§5).** `unsatisfiable_connection` is gone. `spawn_unusable` asks the registered capabilities, whether or not any species is registered. A portal with a side in a region kind the profile has no locomotion for is a new `PortalCheck` reason, `not_applicable`, skipped like `missing_capability`. Walkable land and water touching within a bank's height limit are no `region_seam`. The new limit `no_water_exit` (a warning), for a profile that moves on land and in water, names each water region it can get into (from a land region it can stand on, through banks or connections, or at a spawn point in the water) from which the region graph reaches no land; with banks both ways it fires only when every bank is too high or too far and no connection leads out. It is region-level, like `reachableRegions`.
- **Invariants.** The long runs check, per creature, that it is only in a region kind its profile moves in, on a walkable cell of its own profile, on that region's surface and under no other water surface, with submersion 0 out of the water, and that it changes region only through a connection in a direction it allows or by a jump from where one of its profile's bank crossings starts, as it stood before the tick, to where that crossing lands.
- **Cost.** On Woodland Hollow at medium size, building a walker's navigation takes about 28 ms, as before; an amphibian's about 37 ms, and `check_limits` for it about 44 ms in all. Its banks make 50 crossings over the two banks and the river. The first route across the river takes about 33 ms, since each door it reaches gets its distance field then; later routes about 0.3 ms. The pathfinder keeps each region's exits instead of collecting them on every step of the search.
- **Setting off (§7).** Walking turns the heading towards the next waypoint at `TURN_RATE`, once per tick; while it faces more than `WALK_TURN_LIMIT` (45°) away the creature stands turning, and within it walks on while it turns. Sharp corners are eased the same way. Setting off a half turn away costs at most 0.75 s, well under the stuck watchdog's 5 s. The heading is the creature's own state, so same-seed runs and a save taken mid-turn carry on identically.
- **Not done here:** the debug overlay draws portals but not banks. Routes within one region still ignore that it may split into areas (a separate issue).

### PR 3: content

- **One spawn point in the river, `river-pool`** at (4.25, 14.25), mid-channel on the front stretch about 13 m downstream of the bridge, facing downstream. It is in the generator's layout like the other spawn points, so `world.json` is regenerated; the props stay where they were, since the river already keeps them further away than a spawn point does. The stretch was picked because the river there is wide enough for a swimmer of every size (walkable water 3–4 cells across for a large one), it is clear of the bridge and close to the default camera. A water-only species of any size now registers; walkers and amphibians of every size still stand on every spawn point whose region they move in, and a walker never appears in the river, since spawning picks only spawn points its profile can stand on. The startup checks stay clean.
- **A water-only species gets no `no_water_exit`**, as §4 means: the check is for a profile that also moves on land. A large one gets `disconnected_region` for the river, which its body can't follow under the bridge deck; `river-pool` is in the southern part, and a large amphibian reaches the northern part through the east bank.

### PR 4: renderer

- **One module, three functions.** `packages/renderer/src/locomotion.ts` is the only code that turns a profile into motion. `trackMovement(movement, world, dt)` keeps each creature's speed, measured from how far its drawn position moves between frames on animation time and smoothed over about 0.2 s, and the distance it has moved. `locomotionMotion({ profile, speed, travelled, time, phase, height })` returns a whole-body pose: lift, pitch, roll, yaw, vertical and horizontal scale, and the pivot they turn about. `locomotionTransform(motion, body, matrix)` turns that pose into a matrix about the pivot and returns the lift, including the ground clearance below. Per-part animation replaces those three functions and the profile table behind them.
- **Motion follows the ground, and stops at rest, but for a swimmer's bob.** Each gait cycle is tied to the distance moved, not to time, so steps keep pace with the ground and a faster creature steps faster; a bigger body takes longer strides (a cycle is a base length plus a share of its height). How much of a profile shows eases from nothing at rest to full at 0.3 m/s (`FULL_MOTION_SPEED`), so a creature standing, turning on the spot, eating or sleeping doesn't move; only a swimmer keeps its gentle bob at rest, floating (owner's call). Pausing the simulation stops the view's animation clock (its timescale goes to 0), so a paused creature holds its pose mid-stride, speed and distance kept; the last of the blend towards the newest snapshot is taken without counting it, so resuming starts at the pace it left off.
  - `walk`: a bob up each step (5% of the height), a waddle from side to side over two steps (about 4°), a slight lean into the walk and a small squash as each foot lands, about the feet.
  - `hop`: 30% of each hop on the ground, squashing as it lands and staying low as it gathers for the next; then an arc 35% of its height high, stretching as it leaves and comes down, nose up then nose down; about the feet.
  - `swim`: a sway of about 10° from side to side with a little rocking, weighted by speed, about a point near the front so the tail sweeps like a fish's; and a bob of 3% of the height on its own slower clock, moving or not; no squash.
  - Any other name, or none, draws no motion.
- **Anchors give the pivots.** Every species gets both anchors on its body, placed or by `defaultAnchor` from the drawn bounds, whatever its template, since a walker that can swim sways too. `placePivots` turns them into the two pivots: the feet, and for the swim a point near the front.
  - **This departs from §10's "swaying about the tail"** (owner's call): swaying about the tail swings the head and holds the tail still, which reads less like a fish. The swim pivot lies on the line from the tail through the body, a quarter of the way back from the front (the tail mirrored onto the front, then moved back towards it), so the tail anchor still sets the body's axis and the tail sweeps widest. `buildBody` returns a `DrawingFrame` that maps drawing pixels onto the body; the grey block has one too, as a drawing filling its height. Images are read at `BODY_IMAGE_SIZE` along their longer side, so their pixels are the drawing's. The feet are where the body meets the ground, so wherever they are placed along the body (PR 5 lets the player place them) they sit on the ground line: only their x is taken.
- **How it adds up with cues and the heading.** A body's transform is composed, outermost first: the position lowered by `submersion` × height, the heading, ADR 0018's cue motion about the bottom middle of the body (as before), then the profile's motion about its pivot. Lifts sum; tilts and scales compose, each about its own point. A tilt about the feet is lifted so the body's front, rear or side doesn't dip into the ground, as the eating peck already was, measuring the front and rear from where the feet are. The mesh's matrix is set directly (`matrixAutoUpdate` off), since a pose about two different points is more than a position, a rotation and a scale. Cue sprites rise and fall with the head but don't swing aside with the sway.
- **Submersion and the profile come from the snapshot.** `CreaturePose` gains `animation` and `submersion` from PR 2's `CreatureSnapshot` fields. The interpolator carries the profile of the snapshot the pose blends from, as it does cues, and blends the submersion between snapshots, so a creature sinks smoothly over the tick it enters the water and rises as it leaves.

### Follow-up: center of gravity

- **`feet` is now `centerOfGravity`** (owner's call), in the schema (`BODY_TEMPLATE_ANCHORS`, `ANCHOR_NAMES`, `defaultAnchor`), the renderer's land pivot (`LocomotionPivot` is `"centerOfGravity" | "front"`; `placePivots` takes the center of gravity and the tail) and the Studio. Players read "feet" as one point on a creature that may have several legs, while all that matters is the left–right position the body tilts and squashes about. There is no migration: a library entry with a `feet` anchor no longer loads (AGENTS.md).
- **The hopper has a center of gravity too.** The hop's squash and arc turn about the same pivot as the walk, so a hopper's player places it the same way; before, a hopper always used the default. `BODY_TEMPLATE_ANCHORS` is walker and hopper `centerOfGravity`, swimmer `tail`, and switching between walker and hopper keeps a placed point. The confirm panel no longer has a "no points" case, as every template has one.
- **The default is the middle of the drawn bounds**, both across and up and down, so the dashed marker sits in the body's middle, which reads as a center of gravity. Only x is used, as before: the pivot stays on the ground line, so the motion of a creature at the default is unchanged.
- **The Studio says** "Center of gravity: where the body balances. Only left–right matters; it's what the body rocks and squashes around.", and over the drawing "Click to place (or move) the center of gravity". The drawing guide's "feet on this line" stays: it is about where the drawn body meets the ground (ADR 0011 §3), not the anchor.

### Follow-up: no body templates

- **The template is gone** (owner's call, 2026-10-29), from the schema (`template`, `BODY_TEMPLATES` and `BODY_TEMPLATE_ANCHORS` are removed, with the check that refused another template's anchor), the Studio and the app. Body and Moves overlapped and confused players: the template only preset Moves and picked which single point could be placed, while behaviour came from Moves. A creature that moved on land and swam could place only one of its points, though the renderer uses both, so the other silently stayed at its default.
- **Both anchors are optional for every species.** `ANCHOR_NAMES` is `centerOfGravity` and `tail`; `species.body` is `{ thickness, locomotion: { land: { gait } }, anchors }`, and the land gait is unchanged. A body with `template` fails validation (`unrecognized_keys`). Library entries go to **version 4**, since an entry's format changed; versions 1 to 3 aren't migrated and fail to load with `missing_migration` (AGENTS.md). The species definition stays at schema version 1, as when PR 1 added the body plan: the built-in pack never set a template.
- **Points follow Moves.** The confirm panel is "How does <name> move?" with **On land** (Walks | Hops) and **Swims**, at least one; a new creature starts On land with Walks. On land shows the center of gravity, Swims the tail, both show both, neither none. With both, Points has a radio row for each, and the one picked is what a click on the drawing places (the first by default); the other marker is dimmed. `body-plan.ts` keeps the plain rules: `defaultPlan`, `planAnchors` (ways of moving to points), `canMove`, `locomotionCapabilities` and `planBody`.
- **A hidden point is kept, not released.** Unticking a way of moving hides its point, but a placed one stays in the Studio's plan and comes back if the way of moving is ticked again, as the gait does while On land is off. `planBody` writes only the points the ways of moving use (`activeAnchors`), so a released species never carries a point its player couldn't see. Reset to default clears the points shown and keeps a hidden one; any drawing edit still clears every placed point.
- **The renderer is unchanged**: its pivots already took both anchors, placed or by `defaultAnchor`. The test species in `tools/development` lose their `template` and are otherwise the same.

## Consequences

- A new place to move (air, say) is a capability with a locomotion rule and a profile. A new style within a medium (a land gait now, water styles later) is a body-plan choice and a profile function in the renderer. Neither changes the engine.
- Water-only creatures go hungry until water food arrives. That is visible (the hungry cue) and accepted.
- A world with water needs a bank low and near enough, or an authored exit, for amphibious creatures to get out, and a water spawn point for water-only ones. The per-profile checks report where they are missing: a hint for a water region without a way out, and `no_spawn_point` for a water-only species with nowhere to appear.
- M16's animation is deliberately thin and will be thrown away when per-part animation arrives; keeping it behind one interface makes that cheap.
- Body plans add a field to every library entry, and entries saved before M16 may stop loading. That is accepted until backward compatibility is required.
- M17 starts with its contracts fixed: one edit path, edits in the save, relocation over rejection, whole-region rebuilds, dry runs and inverse edits.
