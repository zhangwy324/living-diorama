# @living-diorama/renderer

Three.js scene: terrain, water, structures, props, creatures, camera and debug overlays.

## Owns

- Everything drawn on screen, including camera state (which never reaches the simulation).
- Interpolation between simulation snapshots.
- The scene built from the simulation's `SceneData`: terrain, diorama base, animated water, structure decks with railings, and props instanced from their model assets, in layers with clipping-plane support ([ADR 0007](../../docs/adr/0007-renderer.md)).
- Creatures: solid bodies extruded from the species `appearance` image ([ADR 0011](../../docs/adr/0011-extruded-creature-bodies.md)), turned to their heading, true to scale, casting shadows from the sun, drawn one tick behind the newest snapshot. A grey block stands in while a drawing loads or if it fails.
- The voxel mesher (ADR 0022 §7, §18, §19, `voxel/mesher.ts`): a pure function, no three.js, from a creature's parts (colour indices in a `Uint8Array` of at most 32³, and sRGB colours) to the typed arrays of one geometry. It culls faces hidden within a part, gives each corner one of four ambient occlusion levels and splits each quad along its more evenly shaded diagonal, and keeps vertices small: `Uint8` positions in the part's own cube units, `Int8` normals, `Uint8` sRGB colour, ambient occlusion and part index, with 16-bit indices when they fit. `voxel/mesher.timing.ts` times it (`pnpm tsx packages/renderer/src/voxel/mesher.timing.ts`).
- Voxel thumbnails (ADR 0022, M20 decisions, `voxel/thumbnails.ts`): `VoxelThumbnails` draws a lone `VoxelPart` at rest, or a whole `VoxelAssembly` in its rest pose, as a PNG data URL, the way prop thumbnails are drawn (`drawThumbnail`: the cue bubbles' three-quarter view in plain daylight, on a transparent background, at most 1024 px square) but with the voxel creatures' own material, so colours, occlusion and glow read as in the world. No icon files. Asynchronous (ADR 0023 §20): asking returns a promise, and queued thumbnails are drawn at animation frames for up to 6 ms each, at least one a frame, so a tray of them doesn't stall one; kept by kind, `id@version` and size, not by object; each thumbnail's geometry, skeleton and render target are freed once read back, while the material's staged copy is kept until `dispose`, so its shader is compiled once, not per thumbnail (#224). The view exposes them as `partThumbnail` and `assemblyThumbnail`; the class and `canvasEncoder` are exported for a page with its own renderer, and the assembly view hands out one on its renderer.
- The Creation Studio's assembly view (ADR 0023 §4, §13, `voxel/assembly-view.ts`): one voxel assembly on a ground grid in a WebGL context of its own, through an orbit camera, rebuilt only when the assembly object changes. It marks the sockets it is given, tints selected parts, shows a see-through part at sockets (`voxel/assembly-points.ts` places sockets and ghosts in the body frame), picks the marked socket or part under the pointer, gives the pointer's ray in the body frame for drag-to-socket, hides the part a ghost would replace, and walks the creature in place with the locomotion profiles. It only draws: which sockets are free and what an edit does are the Studio's and `content-schema`'s.
- Locomotion animation (ADR 0020 §8–§10): a creature's `animation` profile from its snapshot moves its whole body as it moves, a bob and waddle for `walk`, a squash and arc for `hop`, a sway and bob for `swim`, about the ground below its center of gravity (only its x counts) or, for the sway, a point near its front in line with its tail, so the tail sweeps; scaled by its speed from frame to frame and still at rest, but for a swimmer's gentle bob; an unknown profile draws no motion. Its `submersion` lowers the body by that share of its height. `locomotion.ts` is the one module behind this, so per-part animation can replace it; the renderer knows profile names only, no capability IDs or region kinds.
- Cues (ADR 0018 §15): a creature's `cues` from its snapshot pose its body (a gentle bob and peck while eating, with a ring above the head filling with the snapshot's `cueProgress`; lowered by about 10% and breathing slowly while sleeping) and add sprites (rising, leaning z's while sleeping, two at a time at strip size; a round food bubble with a neutral food icon while hungry). Each cue fades in and out. The sprites are drawn procedurally, unlit and over the scene, with thick outlines and sharpened texture sampling, sized in CSS pixels with a minimum so they stay readable in the strip, and pooled. The z's are dark on light by day and light on dark at night, following the snapshot's time of day. `CueSprites.reducedMotion` is a hook for a future reduced-motion setting. A creature hidden from the camera by the terrain or a deck, or clipped away, fades its cues out (`cue-visibility.ts`, a few cheap CPU checks a second per creature); props, trees and other creatures never hide cues. Where creatures bunch up, bubbles and rings are pushed a little apart on screen, easing into place, and sleepers whose z's overlap let fewer rise (`cue-layout.ts`). The renderer knows only the cue vocabulary, no action or need IDs.
- The Creation Studio's body preview and its checks for thin parts and separate shapes ([ADR 0015](../../docs/adr/0015-creation-studio.md) §7): one body turning over a grid, lit like the diorama, in a WebGL context of its own. It can play a locomotion profile in place, about the anchors the player placed, and show a swimmer lowered into a water surface (`setMotion`, `setAnchors`, ADR 0020 §12); the check also gives the drawn shapes' bounds, which anchors default from.
- Prop replacement and editing helpers (ADR 0021 §12): `replaceProps` swaps the props, walkability and region graph for those of a world rebuilt after a prop edit, keeping terrain, water and structures. Models are loaded once per URL and reused, and the old props stay until the new ones are all placed, so nothing flickers; a model load that has not settled after 10 s is drawn as the fallback block, so it cannot hold up later replacements. Replaced instanced meshes are freed, models only on `dispose` (`PropSet`). For the editor, `pickProp` and `pickGround` raycast from a pointer position: the terrain through its heightfield (`terrain-ray.ts`, a few microseconds a ray), props and structures as meshes, skipping hits the layers' clipping planes cut away; props hidden by the terrain or a structure are not picked. `selectProp` rings a placement and follows it through replacements, `previewProp` shows a see-through copy of a placement, and `hideProp` hides the original while it is dragged, through replacements too (`PropEditing`). These only read and draw: edits reach the simulation as commands from the app. For the prop drawer, `propThumbnail(model, size)` draws a model as the cue bubbles draw prop pictures (`drawIcon`) and hands it back as a PNG data URL, kept per model and size (`prop-thumbnails.ts`).
- Camera limits: the orbit target stays over the world and the camera above the terrain. The view hands out and takes back its camera state (`cameraState`, `setCameraState`), so the app can keep one camera per window shape (ADR 0016 §6).
- Pixel density: the diorama view and the body preview follow `devicePixelRatio` when the window moves to a monitor with another scale, up to 2× (ADR 0016 §8, issue #55).
- Debug overlays, hidden until `setDebug(true)`: walkability cells, the region graph and portals, creature routes, structure surfaces as wireframes, an FPS and tick panel, and per creature a label with its action, home, needs and knowledge, with lines to its home and known places and squares for its recent cells (ADR 0018 §15). Walkability and the graph come from the app as `NavigationOverlay` data; routes, needs, knowledge and home come in snapshots, and need and affordance IDs are shown generically by their name after the namespace.
- Render throttling: the display rate while hovered or while the camera moves, about 30 fps otherwise, nothing while the page is hidden.

## Must not

- Mutate simulation state. It may only import `simulation` for types (snapshots, events); data arrives as messages.
- Branch on content IDs.

## Workspace dependencies

`@living-diorama/content-schema`, `@living-diorama/simulation`

Boundary rules are enforced by `pnpm boundaries` (dependency-cruiser) and `pnpm lint`; see roadmap §1.4.
