# @living-diorama/development-tools

Developer tooling: debug world fixtures, the simulation trace viewer and the headless runner.

## Owns

- Fixtures and scripts used only during development and testing, e.g. the `river-crossing` fixture world (a bridge deck stacked over a river), the `pond` fixture (water between two banks, for swimming) and the test species for Woodland Hollow in `src/fixtures/hollow-creatures.ts` (ADR 0020): an amphibian that walks and swims (`fixture:otter`), one that also keeps to the water (`fixture:beaver`, `core:water_preference` 1, #163), a water-only swimmer (`fixture:carp`) and a land-only hopper (`fixture:toad`), each living like the mossling. They stand in for creatures drawn in the Studio, so they stay out of built-in content; `registerHollowCreatures` registers the built-in pack and then them. The voxel species in `src/fixtures/voxel-creatures.ts` (ADR 0022) are the built-in pack's sample voxel creature (`sampleVoxelAssembly()`) in each body plan the dev console offers (`walk`, `hop`, `swim`, `land+swim`), with the size class measured from its cubes and `user:voxel-<plan>` IDs. They stand in for voxel species saved to the library, so they are registered as the app registers those: while the world runs, with `registerSpecies(species, url, "voxel")`, in every fresh world and before a save is restored (`RunOptions.runtimeSpecies`). Nothing reads their URLs.
- The swim and hop scenarios in Woodland Hollow (`src/hollow-swim-hop.test.ts`): the swimmer appears at `river-pool` and stays in the water, the amphibian crosses the river by its banks while a walker takes the bridge, the hopper's gait gives the `hop` profile everywhere, and the three live an evening into the night together with every invariant held.
- The world-edit scenarios (`src/world-edit-scenarios.test.ts`, ADR 0021): edits made while creatures live on, each run with every long-run invariant checked after every tick and after every edit. A tree dropped on a mossling moves it to a walkable cell and it carries on; food a creature is heading for and the home a creature sleeps at are removed, pruned from its memory, and it eats or sleeps elsewhere; trees across the pond fixture's shore exit warn (`water_exit_blocked`, naming `south-shore`) and the newts in the water still get out by the banks; a prop scaled up over a walking mossling's route makes it replan round the prop to the same goal; rocks over the bridge's west end close it (`portal_unusable`) without a failed path or a stuck creature; 60 random edits, undos among them, in the first 30 s (often several between two steps) leave every creature where it may be and give the same world on every run; an undo refused keeps its place in the history; and the meal check lets go of a creature only when no food is left in reach. `src/world-edits.test.ts` covers the engine's edit mechanics step by step.
- The world generator (`src/worldgen`, run with `pnpm worldgen`): it writes the built-in world and a debug map to `out/woodland-hollow.png`, and its tests cover the M2 exit criteria.
- The voxel part importer (`src/voxel-parts`, run with `pnpm parts [--in <dir>] [--out <dir>]`, ADR 0022): it turns each MagicaVoxel `<name>.vox` in the source folder (default `packages/built-in-content/src/woodland-hollow/voxel-parts/vox/`) and its sidecar `<name>.json` into a part definition `<name>.json`, `{ schemaVersion, id, version, name, part }`, in the output folder (default `voxel-parts/parts/` beside it), which it keeps to exactly the sources' outputs. It writes nothing if any part has an error. The sidecar holds what a `.vox` can't: `id`, `version`, `name`, the part `type`, the cube `step` in metres, the `attach` point and the `sockets`, in the part's cubes and axes. `vox-parser.ts` is a pure function over the file's bytes, with no Node APIs, so it can move to content-schema for player imports (M28). It reads versions 150 and 200, one model per file, and refuses a second model, a rotated model, more than 32 cubes along an axis or a file with no palette; a model's translation, materials and layers are ignored. Axes: part X = vox x, part Y (up) = vox z, part Z (right) = size.y − 1 − vox y. Index 0 is empty, and the colours a model uses become the part's list in palette order, identical ones merged. `vox-writer.ts` writes cubes back as a `.vox` (version 150, no scene graph), for parts made in code. A test regenerates the built-in parts and compares them with the committed definitions, as `world.json` is checked against `pnpm worldgen`. The starter parts' first-pass art is made in code (`starter-parts.ts`, one recipe per part, from the shape helpers in `shapes.ts`: boxes, rounded boxes, ellipsoids, superellipsoids and capsules, filled, painted and carved), and `pnpm parts:bootstrap [--out <dir>] [--force] [<part>…]` writes each as a `.vox` and sidecar into the source folder, or only the parts named by file (`round-eye`). It keeps any file already there unless `--force`, which with no part named overwrites every starter part, since the files are the parts' sources once written and the owner refines them in MagicaVoxel; run `pnpm parts` afterwards. `pnpm mossling` (`src/voxel-parts/mossling.ts`) freezes the mossling species' assembly from the built-in parts, placed by hand in `built-in-content`'s `mosslingAssembly`, as `woodland-hollow/assets/voxels/mossling.json`; a test fails while the committed asset differs from what it writes.
- The long-run invariant checks (`src/simulation-run.ts`) and the living-behavior checks of ADR 0018 §20 (`src/long-run-checks.ts`: needs in [0, 1], the decision and interruption bounds, and the meal and sleep checks: a meal at least every 3 hours awake for each creature that can reach food, and asleep for most of the night for each that sleeps at night). The tests, `pnpm soak` and `pnpm sim` use them, with the meal and sleep checks on for Woodland Hollow (`LIVING_BEHAVIOR_CHECKS`) and off for the river-crossing fixture, whose pebbles only wander. The meal check holds only creatures that can reach a spot at some food from where they are first seen (`reachesFood`), so a water-only creature with no food in reach of the water is left out by the world's data, not by its species; the sleep check needs nothing special for one, since it sleeps where it is. `pnpm check` runs a few seeds of a simulated day at 2 Hz, and `pnpm soak [--seeds 32] [--hours 24] [--hz 10] [--creatures 3] [--mosslings-only] [--restores 4] [--compare-minutes 10] [--no-long-gap] [--edits 24] [--jobs <logical cores>]` runs more at the app's rate: `--creatures` mosslings per seed, plus the two amphibians, one water-only swimmer and two voxel creatures (the sample as an amphibian and as a hopper, `SOAK_VOXEL_SPECIES`, registered again before every restore) unless `--mosslings-only`, so the per-capability invariants run on the banks and in the river too, and voxel creatures go through every save and restore. Every mossling must cross the bridge; each seed's line and the summary also count each amphibian species' (the voxel amphibian's too) jumps across a bank and its share of time in the water (#140, #163), the summary with the range of that share over the seeds. Each seed's line also gives the spread of bedtimes and wake times across the creatures the sleep check holds to (earliest–latest, minutes apart and how many; `n/a` when there are none, e.g. when the long-gap restore skipped the night), and the summary the narrowest and widest of those spreads over the seeds (#159). A soak run is one day, so this is a spread across creatures, about one time each, unlike `pnpm sim`'s line per creature, which spreads that creature's times across the nights of its run. A seed whose long gap covers the bedtimes or the waking shows `n/a` for them, since that part of the night is caught up rather than simulated; `--no-long-gap` gives every seed a value. `pnpm soak:quick` is `pnpm soak --seeds 3 --hours 24 --creatures 2`: still a full day and night per seed with every save/restore check and all the extra species, with 2 mosslings instead of 3, in about 40 s instead of about 3 min. `--jobs` seeds run at once, each in a worker thread (`src/soak-worker.ts` running `src/soak-seed.ts`), one per logical core by default; `--jobs 1` runs them one after another on the main thread. The seeds' lines come out in seed order whatever `--jobs` is, and a seed whose run throws or whose worker dies counts as failed. Run the quick soak before a PR that touches simulation, navigation, world content or save/restore, and the full soak once when such a PR changes that logic substantially ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)).
- The save and restore checks of the long runs (`src/save-cycles.ts`, ADR 0019): `pnpm soak` saves each run at random ticks drawn from its seed, through JSON text and `parseWorldSave`, restores it into a fresh world and checks that a restore without a gap carries on exactly as the unbroken run (compared tick by tick for `--compare-minutes`), and that one restore per run after a long gap leaves the world as the catch-up rules say.
- The random edit cycles of the long runs (`src/edit-cycles.ts`, ADR 0021): `pnpm soak` makes `--edits` (default 24, 0 for none) prop edit requests per run at steps drawn from its seed, through `Simulation.applyEdit` between steps as the worker applies them. About one in five is an undo, which applies the inverse of the last edit applied and not undone (a refused undo leaves the history as it was); the rest add, remove, move, scale or turn a random prop, add one where a creature stands so that it is relocated, and about one in five has two or three ops, applied all or nothing. Added, moved and scaled footprints keep 1.5 m from the world's edge, every connection portal, the bridge and every spawn point, so an edit doesn't close the bridge every mossling must cross; banks can still be blocked. Refused edits are fine and are counted by code. After each edit every creature is checked at once as after a tick (a finite position on a walkable cell of its own profile, on its region's surface, inside the bounds), and every long-run check holds on. Every `path_failed` is a failure: the engine's replan after an edit doesn't emit one for a walk whose goal the edit covered or cut off, it interrupts the walk. After each edit the meal check decides afresh which creatures it holds, since an edit may remove the only food a creature could reach (a swimmer's bush by the river) or cut it off from all food (a `disconnected_region` warning); those count as cut off from food, and the creature's clock between meals stops while it is, and runs on from there if food comes back in reach. Edits due on the step right after a restore with catch-up apply a step later, so `catchUpProblems` checks the world as catch-up left it. While an unbroken twin runs beside a restored world, every edit applies to both and must give the same result. Each seed's line gives the edits applied, undos, refusals and relocations, and the summary adds them up with refusals and warnings by code, creatures cut off from food and the median, 95th percentile and maximum `applyEdit` time. A save of an edited world holds the edited definition and the generated-ID counter (ADR 0021 §4), so a restore after edits starts, like any other, from a fresh world of the unedited built-in world and must bring back the world and the counter exactly as saved; then, without a gap, it carries on tick by tick as the unbroken run. Each seed's line and the summary say how many restores were of an edited world.
- The headless runner (`src/sim.ts`, run with `pnpm sim [--world hollow|river] [--seed 1] [--ticks 6000] [--creatures 1] [--hz 10] [--start 12:00] [--save <file>] [--load <file> [--gap <minutes>]] [--species mossling,amphibian,water-amphibian,swimmer,hopper,voxel-walk,voxel-hop,voxel-swim,voxel-land+swim] [--edits <file>] [--no-map]`). `--species` picks who lives in the world from its species table, `--creatures` of each (default `mossling` in Woodland Hollow; the river-crossing fixture has only `pebble`). `--save` writes the world as the run leaves it in the app's save format, and `--load` starts from such a save, `--gap` minutes of wall-clock time after it was made (default 0, an exact resume; 2 or more is caught up). Both run at the app's 10 Hz only, since a save doesn't record its tick rate. It prints every event, a summary and a line per creature (meals, sleep, time hungry, decisions and interruptions, and the earliest and latest of its bedtimes and wake times), and writes every decision and event to `out/sim-<world>-<seed>.ndjson` and the trajectories over the debug map to `out/sim-<world>-<seed>.png`. `--edits <file>` applies world edits during the run (ADR 0021): a JSON array of `{ "tick": n, "edit": { "ops": [...] } }`, the edit as the app's worker takes it (`parseWorldEdit`), or `{ "tick": n, "undo": true }`, which applies the inverse of the last edit applied and not yet undone. `tick` counts steps from the run's start, also after `--load`, and the edit applies before that step; several may share a tick. Each request's result is printed (the ops as applied, relocated creatures and warnings, or why it was refused, and the time it took) and written to the NDJSON as `kind: "edit"` without the time, so a run with the same script stays the same; the summary adds up the edits, and the map shows the world as the run leaves it.
- The rebuild timing (`src/rebuild-time.ts`, run with `pnpm rebuild-time`): the minimum, median and maximum time of a full rebuild of Woodland Hollow, as a world edit costs (ADR 0021), with the built-in content and again with the soak's test species, which add navigation profiles. It times the parts (the world model alone, the world check with the navigation of every registered profile, `WorldState.create`, which adds the pathfinders, and the scene data) and a realistic edit: `WorldState.create`, then per profile the pathfinder data built lazily that the relocation and blocked-exit checks need (every connection's crossings, every region's areas, the banks' crossings), then 20 route queries per profile between fixed walkable cells, standing in for creatures replanning after the edit. Then it times the edit itself in a running world of 20 creatures (mosslings, and with the test species also the two amphibians and the swimmer): `Simulation.validateEdit`, and `Simulation.applyEdit`, which adds reconciling the creatures (relocation, pruning, every creature's replan) to the rebuild, alternating a move, a scale, an add or a removal with its undo.

## Must not

- Be imported by any package or app.

## Workspace dependencies

`@living-diorama/content-schema`, `@living-diorama/extension-api`, `@living-diorama/simulation`, `@living-diorama/built-in-content`

Boundary rules are enforced by `pnpm boundaries` (dependency-cruiser) and `pnpm lint`; see roadmap §1.4.
