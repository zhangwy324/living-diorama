# @living-diorama/development-tools

Developer tooling: debug world fixtures, the simulation trace viewer and the headless runner.

## Owns

- Fixtures and scripts used only during development and testing, e.g. the `river-crossing` fixture world (a bridge deck stacked over a river), the `pond` fixture (water between two banks, for swimming) and the test species for Woodland Hollow in `src/fixtures/hollow-creatures.ts` (ADR 0020): an amphibian that walks and swims (`fixture:otter`), one that also keeps to the water (`fixture:beaver`, `core:water_preference` 1, #163), a water-only swimmer (`fixture:carp`) and a land-only hopper (`fixture:toad`), each living like the mossling. They stand in for creatures drawn in the Studio, so they stay out of built-in content; `registerHollowCreatures` registers the built-in pack and then them.
- The swim and hop scenarios in Woodland Hollow (`src/hollow-swim-hop.test.ts`): the swimmer appears at `river-pool` and stays in the water, the amphibian crosses the river by its banks while a walker takes the bridge, the hopper's gait gives the `hop` profile everywhere, and the three live an evening into the night together with every invariant held.
- The world generator (`src/worldgen`, run with `pnpm worldgen`): it writes the built-in world and a debug map to `out/woodland-hollow.png`, and its tests cover the M2 exit criteria.
- The long-run invariant checks (`src/simulation-run.ts`) and the living-behavior checks of ADR 0018 §20 (`src/long-run-checks.ts`: needs in [0, 1], the decision and interruption bounds, and the meal and sleep checks: a meal at least every 3 hours awake for each creature that can reach food, and asleep for most of the night for each that sleeps at night). The tests, `pnpm soak` and `pnpm sim` use them, with the meal and sleep checks on for Woodland Hollow (`LIVING_BEHAVIOR_CHECKS`) and off for the river-crossing fixture, whose pebbles only wander. The meal check holds only creatures that can reach a spot at some food from where they are first seen (`reachesFood`), so a water-only creature with no food in reach of the water is left out by the world's data, not by its species; the sleep check needs nothing special for one, since it sleeps where it is. `pnpm check` runs a few seeds of a simulated day at 2 Hz, and `pnpm soak [--seeds 32] [--hours 24] [--hz 10] [--creatures 3] [--mosslings-only] [--restores 4] [--compare-minutes 10] [--no-long-gap]` runs more at the app's rate: `--creatures` mosslings per seed, plus the two amphibians and one water-only swimmer unless `--mosslings-only`, so the per-capability invariants run on the banks and in the river too. Every mossling must cross the bridge; each seed's line and the summary also count each amphibian species' jumps across a bank and its share of time in the water (#140, #163), the summary with the range of that share over the seeds. Each seed's line also gives the spread of bedtimes and wake times across the creatures the sleep check holds to (earliest–latest, minutes apart and how many; `n/a` when there are none, e.g. when the long-gap restore skipped the night), and the summary the narrowest and widest of those spreads over the seeds (#159). A soak run is one day, so this is a spread across creatures, about one time each, unlike `pnpm sim`'s line per creature, which spreads that creature's times across the nights of its run. A seed whose long gap covers the bedtimes or the waking shows `n/a` for them, since that part of the night is caught up rather than simulated; `--no-long-gap` gives every seed a value. `pnpm soak:quick` is `pnpm soak --seeds 3 --hours 24 --creatures 2`: still a full day and night per seed with every save/restore check and all three extra species, with 2 mosslings instead of 3, in about 55 s instead of about 12 min. Run the quick soak before a PR that touches simulation, navigation, world content or save/restore, and the full soak once when such a PR changes that logic substantially ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)).
- The save and restore checks of the long runs (`src/save-cycles.ts`, ADR 0019): `pnpm soak` saves each run at random ticks drawn from its seed, through JSON text and `parseWorldSave`, restores it into a fresh world and checks that a restore without a gap carries on exactly as the unbroken run (compared tick by tick for `--compare-minutes`), and that one restore per run after a long gap leaves the world as the catch-up rules say.
- The headless runner (`src/sim.ts`, run with `pnpm sim [--world hollow|river] [--seed 1] [--ticks 6000] [--creatures 1] [--hz 10] [--start 12:00] [--save <file>] [--load <file> [--gap <minutes>]] [--species mossling,amphibian,water-amphibian,swimmer,hopper] [--no-map]`). `--species` picks who lives in the world from its species table, `--creatures` of each (default `mossling` in Woodland Hollow; the river-crossing fixture has only `pebble`). `--save` writes the world as the run leaves it in the app's save format, and `--load` starts from such a save, `--gap` minutes of wall-clock time after it was made (default 0, an exact resume; 2 or more is caught up). Both run at the app's 10 Hz only, since a save doesn't record its tick rate. It prints every event, a summary and a line per creature (meals, sleep, time hungry, decisions and interruptions, and the earliest and latest of its bedtimes and wake times), and writes every decision and event to `out/sim-<world>-<seed>.ndjson` and the trajectories over the debug map to `out/sim-<world>-<seed>.png`.

## Must not

- Be imported by any package or app.

## Workspace dependencies

`@living-diorama/content-schema`, `@living-diorama/extension-api`, `@living-diorama/simulation`, `@living-diorama/built-in-content`

Boundary rules are enforced by `pnpm boundaries` (dependency-cruiser) and `pnpm lint`; see roadmap §1.4.
